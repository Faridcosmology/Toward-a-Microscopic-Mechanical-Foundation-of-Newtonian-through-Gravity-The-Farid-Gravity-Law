Abstract
Newton’s law of universal gravitation provides an experimentally established description of gravitational
attraction in the weak-field, nonrelativistic regime. However, identifying a microscopic
mechanism that connects the internal structure of matter to macroscopic gravitational interaction remains
an important theoretical question.
This article presents the Farid Gravity Law as a proposed mechanical interpretation of Newtonian
gravity within the Tanfarid Quantum Thermodynamic Universe (TQTU) framework. The hypothesis
introduces the Chronon–Plasma Lattice (CPL) as a physical microscopic substrate capable of supporting
collective excitations and transmitting interactions between material bodies.
A central concept is Field-Tension Charge, a proposed effective gravitational source associated
with the complete internal mass-energy of matter, including quark–gluon dynamics, nuclear binding,
electronic contributions, and spin-dependent structure.
The microscopic quantum-rotor Hamiltonian is examined as a starting model. Its harmonic sector
possesses long-wavelength collective excitations, but ordinary local stiffness perturbations generate
interactions that decay more rapidly than Newtonian gravity. An extended lattice Gauss-law construction
provides an inverse-distance static Green function; however, a conventional positive-energy
vector gauge sector does not independently establish attraction between like-sign matter sources.
We therefore identify the principal requirements for a successful derivation: a stable long-range
collective mediator, universal coupling to inertial mass-energy, an attractive interaction derived from
microscopic dynamics, and an independent determination of the gravitational coupling constant.
The proposed framework provides a mathematical research programme for investigating the microscopic
origin of Newtonian gravity while explicitly distinguishing established physical results
from hypotheses requiring further validation.

9 Discussion
The Farid Gravity Law research programme provides a proposed mechanical interpretation of gravitational
interaction, motivated by the internal dynamics of matter and the collective response of a microscopic
lattice substrate. The programme’s central distinction is between identifying a possible microscopic
source of gravity and demonstrating the actual transmission of an attractive long-range force.

Quark–gluon binding energy and nuclear structure contribute to ordinary inertial mass. However, establishing
their role in an independent gravitational charge requires more than identifying their presence
within matter. Similarly, a gapless lattice excitation or Coulomb-type mathematical Green function is
insufficient to establish Newtonian gravitation without a stable attractive interaction and universal matter
coupling.
The present framework therefore remains an open theoretical investigation rather than an experimentally
validated replacement for established gravitational theories. Its scientific value lies in providing explicit
microscopic hypotheses, analytical consistency conditions, and falsifiable mathematical requirements for
further testing.
10 Conclusion
The Farid Gravity Law proposes that Newtonian gravitation may possess a microscopic mechanical foundation
associated with the internal mass-energy of matter and collective interactions within the Chronon–
Plasma Lattice.
The quantum-rotor Hamiltonian provides a mathematical starting point for studying collective excitations,
while the Field-Tension Charge concept offers a candidate description of microscopic gravitational
sourcing.
Analytical investigations show that certain local lattice interactions can generate attractive forces but
fail to reproduce Newtonian inverse-square behavior. Extended long-range field constructions provide
additional possibilities, although the attractive sign, universal coupling, and physical stability remain
unresolved.
The principal objective is therefore to derive, from a consistent CPL–matter Hamiltonian, a universal
Field-Tension Charge and a stable attractive interaction whose macroscopic limit reproduces Newton’s
law without imposing that law as an initial assumption.
The Farid Gravity Law should presently be regarded as a proposed microscopic mechanical research
framework, with its definitive gravitational derivation remaining an open scientific problem.
